## A. First Amendment operation and exact wording

The image of local Amendment Act PDF p. 4 shows Section 8 headed “Amendment of article 174.” Its operative direction is: “For article 174 of the Constitution, the following article shall be substituted, namely:-”. This is a **substitution/replacement**, not merely an unspecified amendment. The operation dataset's generic label `amended` is less precise than the enacted wording.

The replacement Article text, preserving its substantive wording, clause numbering and the separator punctuation visible in the Act scan, is:

> 174. Sessions of the State Legislature, prorogation and dissolution.-(1) The Governor shall from time to time summon the House or each House of the Legislature of the State to meet at such time and place as he thinks fit, but six months shall not intervene between its last sitting in one session and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session. (2) The Governor may from time to time- (a) prorogue the House or either House; (b) dissolve the Legislative Assembly.

Line wraps in the scan have been joined. The hyphen-minus after “dissolution” and after “time to time” is present in the 1951 print and is retained above. The quoted provision ends with the period after “Assembly”; the quotation mark and final punctuation that close Section 8's quoted enactment are not part of Article 174.

The current consolidated text on PDF p. 111 reads:

> 174. Sessions of the State Legislature, prorogation and dissolution.—(1) The Governor shall from time to time summon the House or each House of the Legislature of the State to meet at such time and place as he thinks fit, but six months shall not intervene between its last sitting in one session and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session. (2) The Governor may from time to time—(a) prorogue the House or either House; (b) dissolve the Legislative Assembly.

**Comparison:** the operative words, clause numbering, and sentence content match. The observed differences are typographic dash forms: the 1951 scan prints hyphen-minus separators, while the consolidated edition prints em dashes. No lexical difference was observed. This transcription preserves the distinct source forms rather than normalizing them silently.

## B. Legal dates and Article 394

### Section 1 and commencement

Section 1 on local Act PDF p. 2 reads: “1. Short title.-This Act may be called the Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951.” It contains **no commencement provision** and no delegation to a notification. Therefore no commencement date is being inferred from a commencement clause in the Act.

The official consolidated Constitution's Article 174 footnote on local PDF p. 111 states: “Subs. by the Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951, s. 8, for art. 174 (w.e.f. 18-6-1951).” This is direct official evidence that the Section 8 substitution took effect on **18 June 1951**. The official Act copy prints “18th June, 1951” beneath its title. The date on that line is not itself labelled “assent.” The Lok Sabha Secretariat compilation separately identifies 18 June 1951 as the President's assent date, Gazette notification date, and commencement date; this is corroborative institutional evidence. The First Amendment Gazette page itself was not inspected, so its publication details are not established here from the primary Gazette page.

### Initial effective date

Article 394 on original Constitution PDF p. 250 was visually inspected. It puts Article 394 and a listed set of provisions into force “at once,” then provides that the remaining provisions come into force on 26 January 1950. Article 174 is not in the at-once list. Accordingly, the original Article 174 became effective on **26 January 1950**. This is a direct application of the original Constitution's commencement text, not an assumption from the Constitution's adoption date.

## C. Later Article 174 history

The current official consolidated Constitution on PDF p. 111 reproduces the replacement text and its footnote identifies the First Amendment Act, section 8, as the Article 174 substitution effective 18 June 1951. The Article 174 page has no annotation of a later Article 174 change. Thus the current official consolidation supports the statement that the displayed text is the consolidated text as of **1 May 2026** and that the page's amendment annotation identifies the 1951 substitution. It is not evidence of a legal end date after that snapshot.

The repository's `processed/amendment_operations.json` has 160 operations and exactly one Article 174 record: Amendment 1, section 8, parser operation `amended`. The repository has PDFs for only 18 selected Amendment Acts. This local corpus is **not a complete review of every Amendment Act**. The Article-specific annotation in the official current consolidation is the strongest evidence found for the disclosed change history through its date; no separate page-by-page review of all 106 Acts was performed. Do not describe the local operation search alone as proof of a complete history.
